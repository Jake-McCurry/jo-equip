                          Begin the Adventure
Something real has begun in your life.

When you asked Jesus Christ to come in, you stepped into a personal relationship with God that will
never end. The One who created you now lives within you, and Jesus promises to take you as you are
and make your life extraordinary, Christ-centered, and purposeful.

It is the beginning of a new way of living—with Jesus Himself as your leader.




   My Heart, Christ’s Home
   Many years ago, Robert Boyd Munger described what it felt like when he first invited Jesus
   into his life. He pictured his heart as a home, and Jesus as the welcomed guest who began to
   settle in:

       “One evening I invited Jesus Christ into my heart. What an entrance He made! It was not a
       spectacular, emotional thing, but very real. Something happened at the very center of my
       life. He came into the darkness of my heart and turned on the light. He built a fire on the
       hearth and banished the chill. He started music where there had been stillness, and He
       filled the emptiness with His own loving, wonderful fellowship.”

   Munger then imagined Jesus walking through each “room”—his thoughts, his desires, his
   relationships, his work, his free time, even the private places he felt ashamed to open to
   anyone. In every room, Jesus offered to bring light, order, and life.

   Munger’s story illustrates how Jesus will transform your life inside out as you surrender
   every area of your life to Him and let Him be in charge of it. This booklet shows you how to
   begin this adventure with Jesus as you learn to let Him change you to become all God created
   you to be so that you can do all that He created you to do.



Walking the Path Ahead
The Christian life is more like a marathon than a sprint. There will be days of clarity and days of
confusion, moments of triumph and moments of defeat. Ordinary days and spectacular events.
What remains constant is the presence of Christ and the patient work of His Spirit within you.
He has already given you everything you need to take this journey.

The goal is not merely to finish these pages but to begin living the everyday adventure of walking

He is already with you. Your journey awaits.

Are you certain you would go to Heaven if you died today?

The Bible tells us we can know for sure.

Even after inviting Jesus into your life, you may still wonder. This section is written to give you clear,
biblical confidence that you belong to Christ—now and always.

God’s Free Gift
Jesus Christ paid the full price for all your sins on the cross. Through His death and resurrection,
you have been given direct access to God. God’s perfect justice has been completely satisfied by the
blood of Jesus. He has purchased you—and there are no returns (Hebrews 7:25; 9:12).

Because this gift is so complete, a natural question often arises: “What about the good things I have
done? Don’t they somehow contribute?”

The Bible answers this question with clarity. Read the following verses carefully and write what
each one reveals about who is responsible for your salvation:

Romans 5:8

Ephesians 2:8-9

Titus 3:5-6

1 Peter 3:18

Eternal life is a gift from God. You cannot do anything to earn it.

However, like any gift, you can choose to accept or reject it. It’s your free choice

                       2. Your Identity in Christ
The moment you trusted Christ, your identity changed forever. You are no longer defined by your
past, your performance, or the opinions of others. You are now a child of God, with a new nature
and a secure place in His family.

C. S. Lewis points out, “Mere morality is not the end of life. You were made for something quite
different from that… The idea of reaching ‘a good life’ without Christ is based on a double error.
Firstly, we cannot do it; and secondly… we have missed the very point of our existence.”

Lewis explains that the point of our existence centers on the tremendous inheritance we have in
Christ as His children. Living for Christ is not our duty as His slaves, but our privilege as His sons
and daughters.

Your Inheritance in Christ
As a Christian, you have been given a remarkable inheritance. Take time to read the following
verses and thank God for what is now true of you:

               Your sins are completely forgiven (Colossians 2:13-14)

               You become a child of God—forever (John 1:12)

               Christ actually comes into your life (Revelation 3:20)

               The Holy Spirit is your guarantee (2 Corinthians 1:22)

               Christ gives you His nature (2 Corinthians 5:17)


A New You
“Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, the new creation has come: The old has gone, the new is here!” (2
Corinthians 5:17)
You are now a completely new creation—like a butterfly that was once a caterpillar. You may not
always feel new or act new, but in God’s eyes you are fully new. The Father sees you as the one His
Son purchased with His own blood. You are His royal child (Hebrews 2:9-11).

The apostle Peter describes believers as living stones being built into a spiritual temple, with Jesus
as the cornerstone:

“And now God is building you, as living stones into His spiritual temple … You are a chosen people.
You are a kingdom of priests, God’s holy nation, His very own possession” (1 Peter 2:5, 9).

God says wonderful things about you. In Christ you are:

              Living Stones in His Spiritual Temple

              A Chosen People

              A Kingdom of Priests

              His Very Own Possession

              Citizens of God’s Holy Nation

It does not matter what your background, abilities, appearance, or status may be. You now belong to
Christ’s body with believers from every time and place. You are a full citizen of heaven (Ephesians
2:19).

this change, and He will continue his work throughout your life (Philippians 1:6).

          3. The Holy Spirit — Your Constant
                     Companion
Ready in Heart, but Not Yet Empowered
The disciples were deeply moved after the resurrection and eager to follow Jesus wherever He led.
Yet even after three years of walking with Him, they still lacked the power to live the life He had
called them to live. Enthusiasm alone would not be enough.

Jesus knew they needed more. He told them, “You will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come
upon you” (Acts 1:8). Before leaving, He instructed them to wait for the promise of the Father: “I
will send the Holy Spirit, just as my Father promised” (Luke 24:49, NLT).

The Holy Spirit is given many names in Scripture, yet His primary work in your life is to be your
Encourager and Comforter as you walk with Christ (John 16:7-15). Without Him, living for Christ is
impossible. With Him, you can do all things through the strength He supplies (Philippians 4:13).

The Indwelling Holy Spirit
When you invited Jesus Christ to be your Savior and Lord, He entered your life through the Holy
Spirit—and He will remain with you forever. You are never alone.

Ephesians 3:16-17 says that God strengthens you with power through His Spirit in your inner being,
so that Christ may dwell in your hearts through faith. As Robert Munger pictured it, Jesus desires to
settle into every “room” of your life, bringing light, warmth, and life. Christ lives in you by His Spirit.

Who Is the Holy Spirit?
Though invisible, the Holy Spirit is a real Person—the Spirit of Jesus (John 14:16-21).

Read the following verses and note what they reveal about Him:


  Q: Acts 5:3-4
  Q: John 14:16-17




  Q: 1 Corinthians 6:19




The Bible makes clear that the Holy Spirit is fully God—one with the Father and the Son. As you
yield areas of your life to Him, He brings purpose and meaning. The process is gradual and will
include setbacks, yet “the one who began a good work in you will perfect it until the day of Christ
Jesus.” (Philippians 1:6, NASB).


The Battle Within
Every day, you face influences from within and from the world about you that are pulling you away
from God and going against His will—temptations, anger, addictions, jealousy, dissensions,
hostilities, and other destructive behavior (Galatians 5:19-21). Even good things such as family,
success, and security can come between God and you when they become the center of your life. True
satisfaction comes only when you give Jesus His rightful place: the “throne” of your life (Matthew
6:33).

The apostle Paul writes about the struggle to live for Christ. In Romans 7, Paul writes about his
desire to live for Christ but his repeated failures. He learned that this battle of his flesh (sinful
nature) versus the Spirit can only be won when we surrender our wills to the Holy Spirit. Paul
explains in Romans 8 how he achieved victory through the Holy Spirit.

As long as we live in this body, the conflict between self-centered desires and Christ’s way will
remain. The question is which voice we will follow.

NASB). To be “filled” with the Spirit means to be directed and controlled by Him. A basketball
player willingly places himself under the coach’s direction in order to play well and win the game.
In the same way, we freely choose to place ourselves under the Spirit’s leadership.

passions.

Restoring Fellowship
At times, believers sin and break fellowship with the Holy Spirit (1 John 1:8). This does not mean
your salvation is lost. Jesus paid for all your sins on the cross, once and for all. Broken fellowship
means that you are not abiding in Christ. If it is not mended, you will not experience the abundant
life Jesus promised and will begin to miss His purpose and plan for your life.

You can return and restore your fellowship with God at any moment. The way back is simple:

1. Yield yourself again to God.

2. Confess your specific sins

3. Trust the Spirit that He is in control.

         4. Walking by Faith, Not by Feelings
God’s power to live for Christ and walk in the Holy Spirit is available to us only through faith.


  Q: What does Hebrews 11:6 say about faith?




Faith is a steadfast trust in God that results in action. Living for Christ is not trying to do what Jesus
did in our own strength, but letting Him live His life through us by faith—trusting Jesus enough to
do whatever He tells us to do, exactly as He wants it done.

As we live by faith, Jesus’ love will shine through us. Genuine faith in Christ produces obedience to
God’s Word, the Bible, even though there will be ups and downs (James 2:17).

Your faith will be tested throughout life. Scripture describes the Christian life as a battlefield with
three persistent enemies: the world, the flesh, and the devil. God has given us powerful means to
overcome these enemies.

Overcoming Doubt


   Joni’s Story

       Joni Eareckson was an active, athletic teenager until the day she dove into shallow water
       and broke her neck. In a moment, the future she had imagined was gone. She would never
       walk or use her hands again.

       Although she had trusted Christ as a teenager, the months that followed her injury shook
       her deeply. “In those first few months,” she later wrote, “the promises of God seemed
       anything but true… How could I be expected to believe when everything inside and outside
       of me screamed just the opposite?”

       Through the steady encouragement of a friend and the truth of God’s Word, Joni began to
       choose faith over her feelings. Looking back, she has said that her paralysis was not a cruel
       joke but was used by God’s love. Today she leads an international ministry that shares the
       hope of Christ with people affected by disability.

   Her life reminds us that victorious faith is not the absence of struggle. It is trusting God even
   when we cannot yet see the reason.

             5. God’s Word – Your Road Map
If you received a personal letter from Jesus telling you how much He loves you and offering clear
direction for your daily life, would you read it carefully? Would you return to it often?

That is what the Bible is—God’s own word to you. Just as a new baby needs milk to grow, you need
God’s Word to grow in your new life.

Before You Begin
Obtain a Bible with good references and helps. There are many English translations. You may find a
modern translation easier to understand. Free online Bibles are available at sites such as bible.com
in many languages.


God’s Road Map for Your Life
If you were traveling to a place you had never been, would you rely only on instinct, or would you
use a GPS or reliable map?

While the map will not show you every detail of your travel and what is along the road, it will keep
you from heading in the wrong direction, giving you guidance for your trip.

The Bible provides the “road map” for your journey with God. It teaches you all the truth you need
for your life.

Forming a New Habit
Most of what we do each day is out of habit. In order to make God’s Word part of your life, you need
to make it one of your daily habits. Choose a consistent time (many find mornings helpful) and a
quiet place. Read a section of the Bible and ask God to show you what He wants you to understand
and apply.

Like with any new habit, making God’s Word part of your daily life will take time. Be patient and
persistent. The more you let His Word dwell in you richly, the more you will experience God’s
presence and power in your life.
God’s Viewpoint
The Bible gives you God’s answers to the most important questions of life:

              Who God is

              Your purpose

              How to grow in Christ

              Moral decisions

              Relationships

              Peace and comfort in hardship

              Suffering and death

“For the word of God is living and active and sharper than any double-edged sword… it is able to
judge the desires and thoughts of the heart.” (Hebrews 4:12)


What Fills Your Mind
Every day your mind is flooded with the voices and messages around you—most of them reflecting
the world’s perspective. What you allow to fill your mind will influence your decisions, attitudes,
actions, and direction.

If God’s perspective is to shape your life, you must give your regular time and serious effort to “feed
on” His Word.

Matthew Henry once said: “If you want to get close to God, you must study His Word.”


  Q: How does Colossians 3:16 speak to you personally?

2. Reading God’s Word

Read slowly and thoughtfully. Keep a pen to underline or note what stands out. If you are new to

and together give you key events in the first century from the birth of Jesus to the explosive growth
of the church.

“All Scripture is God-breathed and is useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting and training in
righteousness” (2 Timothy 3:16).

Study can take different forms:

              An individual book

              A single chapter or passage

              A theme (such as love, forgiveness, or faith)

              A key verse

              A central truth of the faith

The goal is not information alone, but insight into who God is and what He wants to do in your life.

One simple way to study is to read a short passage slowly, then ask: What does this show me about
God? About myself? About how I am to live? Write down what you notice.

4. Memorizing God’s Word

Hiding Scripture in your heart gives you truth to carry into every situation. Begin with a few key
verses. Write them on small cards, review them regularly, and speak them aloud. Over time, they
become part of your inner vocabulary.

“I have hidden your word in my heart that I might not sin against you.” (Psalm 119:11).
5. Meditating on God’s Word

Meditation is simply turning a truth over in your mind throughout the day. When stress,
temptation, or uncertainty arises, bring a relevant verse to mind and let it steady you.

The Power of God’s Word to Transform
In the late 1970s, David Berkowitz—known as the “Son of Sam”—was imprisoned after a series of
murders in New York. While in prison, he was given a Bible. As he read, the Word of God began to
confront and change him. In time, he turned to Christ in genuine repentance. Today he serves as a
chaplain’s assistant, a living example of how Scripture can break old patterns and create a new life.

Transformation happens as the world’s perspective is gradually replaced by God’s. The amount of
time you commit to the Bible is probably the single best indicator of your walk with Christ, and the
best predictor of your future growth in Christ.

“Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly” (Colossians 3:16).

     6. Prayer – Your Ongoing Conversation
                    with God
Imagine you are having lunch with a friend when your phone rings. An unfamiliar voice speaks
your name and says, “This is your Heavenly Father. I have been wanting to talk with you.”

How would you respond—surprised, hesitant, grateful, or unsure?

God desires that kind of open conversation with you. He wants you to know Him well enough that
you feel free to come to Him at any time, even when you have sinned. The story of the father and his
younger son in Luke 15:11-32 reveals the heart of God who runs toward those who return to Him.

Prayer is one of the great privileges of belonging to Christ. Through Jesus, you have direct access to
your Father in heaven. He invites you to bring Him whatever is on your heart, and He promises to
listen.


Relationship, Not Rules
When you received Jesus into your life, you entered a personal relationship with God. Every healthy
relationship depends on honest communication. Your Father wants you to seek His guidance, peace,
and companionship at all times and not only when you need something from Him because He
delights in you (Galatians 4:6).

Three truths support a growing life of prayer:

              The Holy Spirit helps you pray

              Faith makes prayer effective

              God’s Word teaches you who He is and how to pray

Jesus said, “Ask and you will receive, and your joy will be complete” (John 16:24).

Knowing Your Father
The more clearly you see who God is, the more you will want to talk with Him. His greatness is far
beyond full human understanding, yet Scripture gives us a glimpse of His character in beautiful and
magnificent ways:

              Sovereign – in complete control
              Eternal – without beginning or end

              All-knowing

              Present everywhere

              All-powerful

              Unchanging

              Holy

              Just

              Loving

              Completely trustworthy

As you consider each of these attributes, see how it affects your view of God and your relationship
with Him. How does it change the way you come to Him in prayer?

Praying with Confidence and Humility
Because you are His child, you are welcome in His presence. The Bible invites you to “approach
God’s throne of grace with confidence, so that we may receive mercy and find grace to help us in our
time of need” (Hebrews 4:16).

At the same time, God calls you to approach Him with humility. Pride resists dependence; humility
opens the way for His help (1 Peter 5:5-6).

True confidence in prayer rests on several truths:

You belong to Him. God hears the prayers of His children. He also hears those who turn to Christ
for the first time in faith.

You remain (abide) in Him. Jesus said, “If you remain in me and my words remain in you, ask
whatever you wish, and it will be done for you” (John 15:7). A life that remains close to Christ
becomes a life of answered prayer according to God’s will.
  Q: What does John 15:7 teach about the connection between abiding and prayer?




  Q: What warning and encouragement do you find in Psalm 66:18-20?




You desire His will. Jesus modeled this attitude in Gethsemane, submitting His own will to the
Father’s purpose (Mark 14:32-40). Scripture gives this assurance: “If we ask anything according to
his will, he hears us” (1 John 5:14).

You wait with patience. Some answers come quickly; others take years to receive answers.
Patience is a sign of a Spirit-controlled life (Galatians 5:22). Many believers can testify to God’s
faithfulness after long seasons of persistent prayer.


Growing in the Practice of Prayer
Beginning the day A simple way to strengthen your relationship with Christ is to turn to Him at
the start of each day. Before the demands of the morning take over, you might say:

“Father, I love You. I want to walk with You today. Lead me in what lies ahead.”

Claiming His promises Carry a verse with you and return to it throughout the day. Proverbs 3:5- 6
is a steady place to begin:

“Trust in the Lord with all your heart, and do not lean on your own understanding. In all your ways
acknowledge him, and he will make straight your paths.”

Restoring fellowship When you become aware of sin, name it, and choose to let Him control your
life. He will then restore you to fellowship with Him. “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just
and will forgive us our sins and purify us from all unrighteousness” (1 John 1:9). The Spirit Himself
helps us in our weakness (Romans 8:26-27).
Thanksgiving and adoration God has already done for you more than you can measure. Many
psalms sing praise and gratitude to God. Praying through them is a great way to worship Him
(Begin with Psalms 95, 107, 145-150).

Bringing requests Your Father wants you to ask. He delights in your requests since it is an act of
faith and obedience. Never quit asking Him for things that are biblical and within His will. He
promises to answer those prayers … in His time!

As you continue in God’s Word and yield to the Spirit, conversation with your Father in prayer will
become more natural. Prayer is not a duty to master; it is a relationship to enjoy.

                 7. Belonging to God’s Family
When you trusted Christ, you were not only given a new identity but also you were placed into a
new family. You now belong to the body of Christ, joined with believers across every generation and
every place.


Christ’s Local Body
All who follow Jesus across time make up the Church, the body of Christ. Scripture also uses the
word “church” for a local gathering of believers.

Jesus promises us: “For where two or three gather in my name, there am I with them” (Matthew
18:20). When we come together with Him as our focus, He is present.


The Example of the Early Church
The book of Acts shows how the first believers lived out this new family life. They regularly met in
homes, shared their lives, and supported one another with deep commitment. The New Testament
letters were written to these early communities, and the same Spirit who guided them continues to
guide the church today.

Their love for one another and their steady witness were so compelling that the message of Christ
spread throughout the Roman world. God worked through ordinary people who took seriously their
life together.

Christian is a necessary part of the body of Christ. Just as every part of the human body has a role—
some visible, some hidden—so every believer has a place and a purpose.

                     8. Living a Life of Purpose
A new identity, walking with the Holy Spirit, faith, the Bible, prayer, and belonging to God’s family
are not ends in themselves. They are means by which we become all that God created us to be so
that we can do all that He created us to do.


Personal Holiness
One of God’s defining attributes is holiness. He is morally pure, righteous, and set apart from all.
The Holy Spirit has set us apart for God and works within us to transform us in the holiness of
Christ to be holy.

Nancy DeMoss writes, “True holiness starts on the inside–with our thoughts, attitudes, values, and
motives–those innermost parts of our hearts that only God can see. It also affects our outward and
visible behavior: ‘Be holy in all your conduct.’”


  Q: What does Peter say in 1 Peter 1:15-16?

Love for Others


  The Holy Spirit actively gives us His supernatural love for others. This love is forgiving rather
  than resentful, patient rather than irritable, and compassionate rather than unsympathetic.
  Corrie ten Boom experienced God’s supernatural love when she chose to love by faith. She was
  able to truly love her enemy—the prison guard who demeaned and abused her.

  This supernatural love is not the work of our human effort. When, in obedience, we surrender
  ourselves to the Holy Spirit and become His willing vessels, His love begins to flow through
  our new nature in Christ.

Investing Your Life
Jesus often spoke of life in terms of investment. He made clear that our days, relationships, and
resources are meant to count for the growth of His kingdom.


  Q: How are you currently investing your life?




In Luke 19, Jesus told of a prince who entrusted money to three servants and later returned to settle
accounts. Two had put the resources to work and were commended. The third had protected what
he was given but produced nothing. God has given us new life in Christ and expects us to live in
such a way that we bear much fruit that remains (John 15:16).

Life on earth is brief. The opportunities to invest your life for Christ are now.


Accountability and Reward
The Bible teaches that every believer will one day stand before Christ and give an account of a life
lived in relationship with Him (2 Corinthians 5:10). This is not a moment of condemnation for
those who belong to Him, but it is a moment of evaluation and rewards.
  Q: What does Paul say in 1 Corinthians 3:11-15 about the way our lives will be tested?




Jesus expects you to invest your life for the greatest possible impact for His Kingdom. Like the
prince who rewarded the servants who multiplied their investments, Jesus eagerly looks forward to
rewarding you.

The motivation is not earning God’s love, for that is already secure in Christ. We invest our lives as
our grateful response to His unconditional love and immeasurable grace.

Expanding Your “Territory”
In 1 Chronicles 4, there is a brief story of a man named Jabez. He asked God for “expanded territory”
(1 Chronicles 4:9-10) because he wanted to invest his life for the greatest return. In the 19th century,
an American evangelist, D. L. Moody, expressed a similar desire in his statement: “The world has yet
to see what one man who is totally sold out to Jesus Christ can accomplish. By God’s grace, I want to
be that man!”


  Q: What area of influence or usefulness are you asking God to grow in your life?




Ambassadors for Christ
Paul writes, “We are ambassadors for Christ” (2 Corinthians 5:20). An ambassador represents a
sovereign authority in a foreign state. In the same way, Jesus, your King, has commissioned you to
represent His Kingdom in this world. You are Christ’s witness and God’s choice to reach others for
Him.

Your witness takes two primary forms: Your walk and your talk

Many people around you quietly carry burdens, disappointments, or hurts. Some are searching for
meaning and purpose. You may be the person through whom they first encounter Jesus.
