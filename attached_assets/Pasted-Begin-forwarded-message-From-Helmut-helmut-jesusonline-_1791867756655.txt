Once the book is web pages, you can do far more than a PDF coating. The goal is still the same: easy to start, easy to stay, easy to come back. Add only what serves that.
1. Make the path feel like a book, not a blog
You already designed this. Keep it.

Intro first, then linked titles
Each title opens that reading
Bottom of every reading: Next reading · Back to the list
Flat site menu
That is interactivity: choice without getting lost.
Add one quiet extra: a thin
progress bar at the top of each reading (“3 of 9”) so they know where they are. That raises finish rates more than page-flip animation.
2. Make the page comfortable on a phone
This is what people feel as “enjoyable.”

16–18px type, short paragraphs, generous line spacing
A Aa control (smaller / larger)
Optional dark page
3–8 min read under the title
No interstitials, no full-width ads between paragraphs
WordPress: many themes already have this. Plugins such as a reading-progress bar and a simple “estimated read time” block are enough.
3. Let them move inside a long reading
If a single reading runs past ~800 words:

Short subheads
A small sticky contents for that reading only (not a second site menu)
Optional “Back to top”
Do not paginate one reading across “page 1 of 4.” That is the old magazine trick and it loses people.
4. Let them listen
A Listen button (browser speech or a recorded voice) helps commuters and tired eyes. Keep it optional and under the title, never required before they can read.
5. Let Scripture be tappable
If verses sit in the text:

The reference opens the NET Bible in a new view, or a clean overlay
Do not dump them into a crowded Bible site with ads
That one link makes the page feel alive without turning it into an app.
6. Save place and notes (only if they want an account)
Without a login you can still:

Remember last reading in this browser (simple and worth doing)
Offer Send this page to yourself (you already have this)
With a light login (email magic link):

Resume where they stopped
A few private notes per reading
“Send my notes to me”
Do not require an account to start. Require it only to
keep work.
7. Light interaction inside a reading
Use sparingly—one per reading is plenty.

A single reflection question with a box they can type in (saved locally or emailed)
“Talk to God about this” as a short prompt, not a quiz
One related reading link: “If this stirred fear, open I’m afraid…”
Skip scored quizzes, badges, and streaks. Those feel like a course. This is a companion.
8. What usually makes it less enjoyable

Flipbook overlay on HTML text
Nested menus
Auto-playing video above the first paragraph
“Read more” under an already-linked title
Locking chapter 2 until chapter 1 is finished
Chat widgets that pop on load
A simple stack for your site

Need
Do this

Explore the book
Linked titles + next / back

Know where I am
“3 of 9” + progress bar

Comfort
Big type, short paragraphs, optional listen

Scripture
Tap the verse → NET Bible

Come back tomorrow
Email-to-self + last-page memory in the browser

Go deeper
One related link, not a new menu

You do not need a special “interactive book plugin” for most of this. You need clean pages, a progress cue, working chapter links, and a way to return. That is already the architecture of the opening pages you have
written. Build those first. Add listen, notes, and verse popovers only after that path is live.
