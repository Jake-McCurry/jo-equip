Holy, holy, holy is the Lord who commands armies!
The whole earth is filled with his glory.”
— Isaiah 6:3

Power can govern a world. Holiness tells us what kind of God governs it.

If the last chapters showed that God is and that God reigns, this chapter shows that His being and His rule are morally perfect. He is not merely strong. He is pure. He is not merely present. He is set apart. Nothing in Him is mixed with evil, and nothing in His ways is crooked. The seraphim do not cry “mighty” three times before the throne. They cry holy.

This category gathers five attributes of that moral glory.

  • Holy — He is perfectly pure and set apart from all sin.
  • Righteous — All His ways and decisions are morally perfect.
  • Just — He renders perfect fairness in every judgment.
  • Faithful — He never fails to keep His promises or His covenants.
  • Absolute Truth — He is the source and unchanging standard of all truth.

These are not five moods of a changing deity. They are one integrity, seen from five sides. Holiness is the fire at the center. Righteousness is that holiness expressed in all He does. Justice is that holiness applied without favoritism. Faithfulness is that holiness kept across time, so that what He has spoken He will perform. Truth is that holiness made known, so that reality itself answers to Him and not He to us.

This chapter answers a question the others cannot answer: What is God like in His moral being, and how does He make right? A God of raw ability could rule and still be unsafe. A God of affection without this purity could comfort and still mislead. Holiness will not permit either distortion. It is the unchanging standard of what is right, true, and pure in a world that has learned to call compromise wisdom.

To approach these pages is to take off one’s shoes. Holiness first humbles. It names our sin without flattery. Then it steadies. The same God who cannot look upon evil has made a way, in Christ, for sinners to live before His face. We do not lower the standard in order to come near. We come near because the Holy One has come near to us.

The attributes that follow should be read as one burning light. Let them search you. Let them cleanse your idea of God. Let them teach you to love what He loves and to call evil by its name—because He is holy, and He does not change.

God Is Holy

He is perfectly pure and set apart from all sin.

Because God is holy, I will give Him wholehearted reverence and devotion.

God Is Righteous

All His ways are morally perfect.

Because God is righteous, I will trust that what He does is right.

God Is Just

He judges with perfect fairness.

Because God is just, I will leave justice and judgment with Him and deal fairly with others.

God Is Faithful

He keeps every promise He has made.

Because God is faithful, I will believe His promises and know He will not forsake me.

God Is Absolute Truth

He is the source and standard of all truth.

Because God is absolute truth, I will build my life on what He has said.

These attributes of holiness do not give us a colder God. They give us a God whose judgments can be trusted when every other measure shifts. His ways are not moods. His promises are not drafts. What He has said remains true whether the world receives it or not.

In a world that will not call evil by its name, holiness steadies the conscience and restores moral sight. Sin is not a small error against a flexible rule. It is offense against the One who is light, and in whom there is no darkness at all.

That same light has shone upon us in Christ. The Holy One did not lower the throne in order to save. He kept every demand of righteousness and then bore, in our place, the judgment our unholiness deserved. So we do not flee His purity. We hide in it. And we begin, by His Spirit, to love what is clean, to keep our word, to tell the truth, and to do right when it costs us—because He is holy, and He has made us His.
