He is the source and unchanging standard of all truth.

It Is Impossible for God to Lie (Titus 1:2; Hebrews 6:18)
Paul writes of “God who does not lie.” Hebrews adds that it is “impossible for God to lie.” This is not a policy God adopted. It is His nature. He cannot deceive because He cannot become untrue to Himself. What He promises is as sure as who He is.

Your Word Is Truth (John 17:17; Psalm 119:160)
“Set them apart in the truth; your word is truth.” “Your instructions are totally reliable; all your just regulations endure.” God’s speech matches God’s being. Scripture is not a useful approximation of reality. It is truth that sanctifies, endures, and judges every other claim.

I Am the Truth (John 14:6)
“I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me.” Truth is not finally a theory. It is a Person. Every lesser claim must stand or fall before the Son. To know Him is to come to the Father. To refuse Him is to refuse the Truth itself.

The Father of Lies (John 8:44)
“He does not remain in the truth, because there is no truth in him. Whenever he lies, he speaks according to his own nature, because he is a liar and the father of lies.”
Falsehood has a source, and it is not God. All lying is rebellion against the One who is true. The contrast is not polite. It is light against the dark.

Faithful and True (Revelation 19:11)
“Then I saw heaven opened and here came a white horse! The one riding it was called Faithful and True.” The last word over history will not be rumor, spin, or a revised story. It will be Christ, whose name is Truth, judging and making war in perfect alignment with what is real.

Daily Impact

Truth is not a tool God uses when it helps. It is who He is. He cannot lie. His Word is truth. Christ is the Truth. That gives the soul a place to stand when feelings contradict the promise, when culture revises what God has named, and when our own hearts prefer a softer tale.

Daily this attribute calls us to honest speech, to Scripture over impulse, and to Jesus over every rival claim about the way to the Father. It also comforts. We are not left to invent reality. The God who made us has spoken, and the Son who is Truth has come. Walking in the light is not grim. It is freedom from managing a lie.

Because God is absolute truth, I will build my life on what He has said.

Questions for Personal Application

  1. Where am I still treating God’s Word as advice I may edit, rather than as truth that stands over me?
  2. What smaller story—about God, myself, or someone else—do I need to release because it does not match what He has said?
  3. How should “I am the way, and the truth, and the life” change a decision I am facing this week?
  4. In what conversation am I tempted to shade the truth, and what would honesty look like if God cannot lie?
  5. Whom can I point to Christ as the Truth this week, rather than to a theory, a mood, or a private version of God?

Word Pictures of God’s Truthfulness

The Light That Does Not Pretend
Darkness hides. Dimness flatters. Light tells the truth about the room. God is not a kinder gloom. He is Truth: what is, is; what is not, is not. We are not asked to decorate the shadows. We are asked to come into the light.

The Word That Cannot Unsay Itself
People revise yesterday’s sentence when it becomes costly. God cannot. “It is impossible for God to lie.” His speech does not have a back door. The promise remains the promise.

The Face of Truth
Maps can be true and still be paper. Jesus does not merely carry true statements. He is the Truth. To come to Him is to come to reality. To refuse Him is to refuse the way things actually are.

Reflection

These pictures remind us that truth is not a costume God puts on. He is light that will not flatter the room. He is a Word that cannot unsay itself. He is a Face—Jesus, the Truth—not a theory we rearrange. Because He is absolute truth, we may stop managing the story. We come into the light. We take Him at His speech. We come to the Son if we want the Father. Falsehood is not a small tool. It is allegiance to another father.

  1. What corner of my life am I still asking the Light to pretend not to see?
  2. Which word of God have I been treating as revisable—a sentence He might unsay if I wait long enough or hurt enough?
  3. Am I coming to the Face of Truth, or only to ideas about Him that I can still edit?
