He knows all things perfectly.

Searched and Known (Psalm 139:1–6, 13–16)
“O Lord, you examine me and know. You know when I sit down and when I get up; even from far away you understand my motives.… Your eyes saw me when I was inside the womb. All the days ordained for me were recorded in your scroll before one of them came into existence.” God’s knowledge is not a distant file. It is intimate and complete. He knows the inner life and the whole span of a person’s days.

He Knew Nathanael (John 1:47–49)
“Look, a true Israelite in whom there is no deceit!” “Before Philip called you, when you were under the fig tree, I saw you.” Christ knows character and the private moment. Nothing about a person is offstage to Him.

He Knew Peter’s Fall and His Restoration (Luke 22:31–34; John 21:15–19)
Jesus told Peter he would deny Him three times before the rooster crowed. He also prayed for him and, after the resurrection, restored him. God’s knowledge includes our failures. It also includes His purpose to raise us. He is not surprised, and He is not finished.

He Named Cyrus Before He Was Born (Isaiah 44:28–45:4)
“Who commissions Cyrus, the one I appointed as shepherd to carry out all my wishes.” About 150 years before the man appeared, God named him and declared his work. His knowledge of future people and events is not guesswork. It is complete.

Nothing Hidden (Hebrews 4:13; 1 Samuel 16:7)
“And no creature is hidden from God, but everything is naked and exposed to the eyes of him to whom we must render an account.” “The Lord does not look at the things people look at. People look at the outward appearance, but the Lord looks at the heart.” Every motive, secret, and circumstance is already known to Him. We do not introduce ourselves to God. We come to the One who already sees.

Daily Impact

God’s omniscience brings both holy seriousness and deep comfort. Nothing is hidden, so hypocrisy is pointless and secret sin is never truly secret. At the same time, the One who knows us best still loves us in Christ. We do not have to explain ourselves perfectly, impress Him, or fear that some forgotten detail will surprise Him. He already sees the whole of our story. This truth steadies decision-making, because He knows the path we cannot see. It steadies suffering, because nothing that touches us is unknown to Him. Daily it calls us to walk in the light—confessing quickly, praying honestly, and resting in the God who understands us better than we understand ourselves.

Because God is all-knowing, I will take every question and concern to Him.

Questions for Personal Application

  1. What secret fear, failure, or motive do I need to bring into the light before the God who already sees it?
  2. How should His perfect knowledge change my prayer when I do not even know what to ask?
  3. In what decision am I anxious because I cannot see the future, and how can I trust the One who can?
  4. How does Jesus’ knowledge of Peter’s fall and his restoration give me hope for my own weakness?
  5. Whom can I remind this week that they are fully known—and still loved—by God?

Word Pictures of God’s Omniscience

The Light That Fills Every Corner
Picture a room where no shadow can hide. Every object is visible, even what is tucked away. God’s knowledge is that light. Secrets cannot retreat from His sight. He does not guess at what we bury. He sees it.

The Watcher Under the Fig Tree
Think of a quiet moment when you believed no one saw you. Then you discover Someone was already there, knowing your heart before you spoke. God is that Watcher—not as a spy, but as the One who sees in order to call and restore.

The Open Book
Imagine a book in which every page of your life is fully known—every thought, word, motive, and day. Nothing is missing. Nothing is smudged. God does not skim. He knows the whole book. That knowledge is not a cage. It is the gaze of the One who already understands you, and who still comes near.

Reflection

These pictures remind us that we are never unknown. That could be terrifying if God were only a Judge. In Christ it becomes a refuge. The God who knows every failure also knows how He will finish His work in us. Because He is all-knowing, we can stop managing our image and start walking in truth. We can ask for wisdom without pretending we already have it. We can confess without rehearsing a defense. We are fully seen—and in Jesus, fully received.

  1. What have I tucked into a corner, as if shadow could keep it from the Light that fills the room?
  2. When I remember the Watcher under the fig tree, do I receive His seeing as a threat—or as the gaze that calls and restores?
  3. If my life is an open book to God, where am I still managing the cover instead of walking in the truth He already knows?
