Nothing is too difficult for Him.

He Spoke, and It Was (Genesis 1:1–31; Psalm 33:6–9; Isaiah 40:26)
“By the Lord’s decree the heavens were made.… For he spoke, and it came into existence.” “Lift up your eyes and look to the heavens. Who created all these heavenly lights?” Creation is the first great proof that nothing is too difficult for Him. He formed all things from nothing and still upholds them by the word of His power.

Is Anything Too Difficult? (Genesis 18:14; 21:1–7)
“Is anything too difficult for the Lord?” Abraham and Sarah were past the years of childbearing. Sarah laughed. Then Isaac was born. God’s power is not limited by empty wombs or natural law.

The Sea Obeyed Him (Exodus 14:21–31)
The Lord “drove the sea back by a strong east wind all that night and turned the sea into dry land.” Israel crossed. The waters returned. Nature is not a rival god. At His command the barrier became a path, and the path became judgment.

Nothing Is Too Hard for You (Jeremiah 32:17, 27; Ezekiel 37:1–14)
“Oh, Lord God, you did indeed make heaven and earth by your mighty power and great strength. Nothing is too hard for you!” “I am the Lord, the God of all humankind. There is, indeed, nothing too difficult for me.” Exile looked like a graveyard. God asked Jeremiah the same question He asked Abraham, then showed Ezekiel a valley of dry bones that lived. He can restore what appears utterly dead.

Lazarus and the Empty Tomb (John 11:43–44; Matthew 28:5–6)
“Lazarus, come out!” The dead man came out. “He is not here, for he has been raised, just as he said.” Jesus calmed storms, multiplied bread, and healed the incurable. Then He walked out of His own grave. Death itself is not too difficult for God.

Daily Impact

A right view of God’s omnipotence replaces despair with expectant faith. When we face illness, closed doors, stubborn hearts, or tasks far beyond our strength, we remember that the God who spoke worlds into being is not strained by our need. This truth does not promise that every desire will be granted on our timetable. It does promise that no situation is too hard for Him.

Daily, it moves us from frantic self-effort to bold prayer and quiet obedience. We work diligently, yet we rest, because the outcome does not finally rest on our limited power. Omnipotence invites courage, perseverance, and worship of the One for whom nothing is impossible.

Because God is all-powerful, I will bring Him every need.

Questions for Personal Application

  1. What “impossible” situation in my life do I need to bring to the God for whom nothing is too difficult?
  2. How should God’s omnipotence change the way I pray this week?
  3. In what area am I still relying on my own strength rather than asking God to do what only He can do?
  4. How can remembering the resurrection keep me from treating death, delay, or defeat as ultimate?
  5. Whom can I encourage this week with the question God asked Sarah: “Is anything too difficult for the LORD?”

Word Pictures of God’s Omnipotence

The Voice That Creates
Imagine a word spoken in the dark, and light appears. No tools, no struggle, no delay—only command and reality. God’s power is that Voice. He does not strain to act. He speaks, and it is so.

The Arm That Parts the Sea
Picture water standing like walls while a people walk through on dry ground and an army waits behind. No human strength could hold that sea. God is the Arm that opened it and closed it again. What blocked His people became, at His command, a path.

The Breath That Raises Dry Bones
See a valley of scattered bones—silent, hopeless, finished. Then the breath of God enters, and the dead stand. What looks beyond repair to us is not beyond His power to restore.

The Hand That Raises the Dead
Think of a tomb sealed four days, and then a voice calling a name. Life returns at His command. God’s power reaches past the last human limit. Death itself is not too difficult for Him.

Reflection

These pictures remind us that our limits are not His limits. The God who created from nothing, opened the sea, gave a child to the barren, and raised the dead is the same God who hears our prayers today. Omnipotence does not make our obedience unnecessary; it makes our hope reasonable. Because nothing is too difficult for Him, we can stop measuring His ability by our weakness. We can ask largely, wait patiently, and walk forward in the strength of the One whose power has no end.

  1. Where am I still measuring God as if He must strain—tools, time, and effort—rather than trusting the Voice that speaks and it is so?
  2. What barrier looks to me like a standing sea, and how would I walk if I believed His arm could open a path?
  3. What have I quietly named “finished”—too far gone, too late, too dead—that I now need to bring to the God who gives breath to dry bones and calls the dead by name?
