He is fully present in every place at all times.

David’s Awe in Psalm 139 (Psalm 139:7–12)
David declares, “Where can I go to escape your presence? Where can I flee to escape from your sight?” (Psalm 139:7). He explores every extreme — ascending to heaven, descending to Sheol, traveling to the far side of the sea, or hiding in darkness — and finds that God is there, guiding and holding him with His hand. This intimate psalm reveals God’s complete and personal presence everywhere.

God Fills Heaven and Earth (Jeremiah 23:23–24)
The Lord asks, “Am I only a God who is near, and not a God who is far away?... Do I not fill heaven and earth?.” This declaration confronts any attempt to hide from God or limit Him to one location, affirming that He is intimately near yet sovereignly transcendent over all creation.

Solomon’s Prayer of Dedication (1 Kings 8:27; 2 Chronicles 6:18) Even as Solomon built the magnificent temple, he acknowledged, “But will God indeed live on the earth? Look, if the sky and the highest heaven cannot contain you, how much less this temple I have built!.” This highlights that no structure or place can confine the God who is present everywhere.

Paul’s Proclamation in Athens (Acts 17:27–28)
Paul told the Athenians that God is not far from any one of us, “for in him we live and move about and exist.” This truth underscores that God’s presence sustains every person and every moment, whether recognized or not.

Jesus’ Promise to His Disciples (Matthew 28:19–20)
After His resurrection, Jesus commanded His followers to make disciples of all nations and assured them, “And remember, I am with you always, to the end of the age.” Through the Holy Spirit, the omnipresent Christ remains fully with every believer, wherever they may be.

Daily Impact

Omnipresence turns ordinary locations into holy ground. Heaven is not the only place God is, and the sanctuary is not the only place He can be found. He is with us in the kitchen and the hospital, on the road and in the night watch, in crowded rooms and in the hour no one else sees.

This truth cuts two ways. We cannot hide. We also cannot be abandoned. The God who fills heaven and earth is not confined to our best moods or our sacred buildings, and He is not absent from our worst days.

Daily this calls us to walk as those already in His sight—honest, watchful, and unafraid. Prayer need not wait for a quieter room. Courage need not wait for a stronger feeling. The risen Christ has said He is with us always. That promise does not shrink the universe. It fills the place where we now stand.

Because God is present everywhere, I will live this day knowing I am never truly alone.

Questions for Personal Application

  1. Where this week have I been living as if God were in some other place than the one I now occupy?
  2. What hidden corner of my life still treats His presence as a threat rather than a refuge?
  3. How should the truth that He fills heaven and earth change the way I pray in ordinary hours—work, travel, waiting, the middle of the night?
  4. In what lonely or frightening situation do I need to remember Christ’s word, “I am with you always”?
  5. Whom can I remind this week that they are not abandoned—that the God who is everywhere is also with them?

Word Pictures of God’s Omnipresence

The Place You Cannot Leave
David tries every escape: heaven, the grave, the far side of the sea, the cover of night. In each place he finds the same Lord, still there, still holding him. God’s presence is not a room we enter. It is the truth that there is no room without Him.

The House That Cannot Hold Him
Solomon built a temple and then confessed that heaven itself could not contain God. The house was not a cage for the Lord. It was a place to remember that the Lord is already everywhere the worshiper will go when he leaves.

The Near God and the Far God
Some imagine a God who is only close—small enough to manage. Some imagine a God who is only distant—great enough to ignore. Jeremiah will not allow either. He is near, and He is far. He fills heaven and earth. Presence is not the shrinking of God. It is the greatness of God arriving at every point.

Reflection


These pictures remind us that presence is not a feeling we must first produce. God is already there. We cannot climb high enough to reach a Godless height, or sink far enough to find a Godless depth. He is not only near, as if He were small, and not only far, as if He were absent. He fills heaven and earth. And the Christ who sends His people does not wave from a distance. He goes with them. Because He is ever-present, hiding is impossible—and so is abandonment. The place where you now stand is already before His face.

  1. Where have I been trying to leave the Place I cannot leave—fleeing God in work, in shame, or in the dark—only to find that He is already there?
  2. Have I been treating God as only near (a God I can manage) or only far (a God I can ignore), instead of the Lord who fills heaven and earth?
  3. What ordinary or frightening place this week needs to be lived under the traveling promise: “I am with you always”?
