All His ways and decisions are morally perfect.

The Rock Whose Work Is Perfect (Deuteronomy 32:4)
“As for the Rock, his work is perfect, for all his ways are just. He is a reliable God who is never unjust, he is fair and upright.” This is the name of the attribute. God does not have righteous moments. He is righteous. Blessing, discipline, and deliverance all flow from a character that does no wrong.

Righteous in All His Ways (Psalm 145:17; Psalm 119:137)
“The Lord is just in all his actions, and exhibits love in all he does.” “You are just, O Lord, and your judgments are fair.” Righteousness is not a rare decision. It marks every path He takes, every word He speaks, and every judgment He renders.

Shall Not the Judge Do What Is Right? (Genesis 18:25)
“Does the Judge of the whole earth not do what is right?” Abraham does not lecture God. He appeals to who God already is. The Holy One will not sweep away the righteous with the wicked. He cannot deny Himself.

Righteousness Displayed at the Cross (Romans 3:25–26)
God publicly displayed Christ “to demonstrate his righteousness.… He did this to demonstrate his righteousness at the present time, so as to be just and the justifier of the one who lives because of Jesus’ faithfulness.” At the cross, righteousness is not set aside in order to save. Sin is judged. Sinners are justified. Love does not cancel the moral perfection of God. It fulfills it.

He Comes to Judge in Righteousness (Psalm 96:13; Revelation 19:11)
“He will judge the world fairly, and the nations in accordance with his justice.” “Then I saw heaven opened and here came a white horse! The one riding it was called Faithful and True, and with justice he judges and goes to war.” The last judgment will not be a new personality. It will be the same righteous Lord, seen without remainder. He will do what is right.

Daily Impact

Righteousness gives the conscience a home. In a world of crooked scales and shifting rules, God does not become unjust in order to be kind, and He does not become unkind in order to be just. All His ways are right.

That steadies us when His providence hurts and when His timing is slow. It also searches us. If He is righteous in all His ways, we cannot call our own crooked ways harmless.

Daily this attribute teaches us to love what is fair, to keep our word, to refuse favoritism, and to bring our guilt to the cross—where God remained just and became the justifier of those who belong to Jesus. We do not need a smaller standard. We need the righteous God who has made a righteous way.

Because God is righteous, I will trust that what He does is right.

Questions for Personal Application

  1. Where am I doubting that the Judge of all the earth will do what is right in a situation I cannot settle?
  2. What crooked way in my speech, work, or relationships needs to be named and left, because He is righteous in all His ways?
  3. How should the cross—God just and the justifier—change the way I deal with my own guilt this week?
  4. In what decision am I tempted to choose what works over what is right?
  5. Whom can I treat with the fairness I have received from the righteous God, instead of with partiality or payback?

Word Pictures of God’s Righteousness

The Rock That Does Not Lean
A leaning stone cannot bear a house. Moses called God the Rock whose work is perfect. Righteousness is not a pose God strikes. It is the unbent weight-bearing character of all He does. Nothing in Him slants toward wrong.

The Plumb Line
Builders drop a line to see what is true to square. Walls that please the eye can still be crooked. God’s righteousness is that line. He does not measure Himself by us. We are measured by Him. What is off remains off until it is made right.

The Path That Never Bends into Wrong
Some roads look useful and still lead into harm. “The Lord is righteous in all his ways.” His path does not detour into deceit, cruelty, or partiality in order to arrive at a good end. The way He walks is as holy as the end He seeks.

Reflection

These pictures remind us that righteousness is not a mood of God and not a tool He picks up when the world is watching. He is the Rock that does not lean. He is the plumb line by which every wall is tested. He is the path that never bends into wrong in order to reach a good end.

Because He is righteous, we may trust His ways when they hurt and refuse our own ways when they merely work. The cross has not lowered the line. It has upheld it—and then brought the guilty near.

  1. Where have I been asking the Rock to lean—hoping God will be a little less right so that I can be a little more at ease?
  2. What in my life looks straight to the eye but would not stand beside His plumb line?
  3. On which path am I tempted to bend into wrong for a result I call good, instead of walking the way that matches who He is?
