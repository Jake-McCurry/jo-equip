“Let the one who boasts boast in this: that he understands and knows me.”
— Jeremiah 9:24

We have not reached the end of God. We have only reached the end of these pages.

We stop here on purpose—not because God has been fully measured, but because He has been truly glimpsed.

The aim of this book was never to close the subject, as if twenty attributes could contain the Living One. The aim was to open the eyes. We have stood before the God who is, the God who reigns, the God who is holy, and the God who gives Himself. If these four glimpses have reached the heart, something quieter than information has begun: the heart has learned again that God is greater than our fear, purer than our excuses, and nearer than our unbelief.

Do not leave this vision in the mind alone.

The self-existent God frees you from the exhausting fiction that you must hold yourself together. You are not the source of your own life. You receive breath, time, and mercy from the One who depends on nothing. Begin each day there. Give thanks before you strive. Pray as His creation, not as a rival.

The sovereign God frees you from the other fiction—that the world, or your will, must finally govern the story. He is able. He knows. He is present. He is free. You may bring Him what you cannot carry. You may release what you cannot rule. Rest is not denial. It is confidence in the King.

The holy God will not let you call that rest indifference. His light searches the hidden room. Let it. Confess what He names. Keep your word. Tell the truth. Love what is clean. Holiness is not the enemy of peace. It is the reason peace is safe.

The God who is love will not let you keep what you have seen. Mercy received becomes mercy given. Grace received becomes an open hand. Goodness received becomes kindness in ordinary rooms. Wisdom received becomes patience with those still finding their way. The love of God, once it has reached you, is not a private treasure. It is a calling.

This is what it means to live in the light of His majesty. It is not a mood reserved for the last page of a book. It is a way of seeing that follows you into work, into suffering, into the church, and into the faces you are given to love. When trouble comes, you do not have to invent a smaller god who will be easier to manage. You return to the whole vision: the Independent One, the reigning King, the Holy Judge, the Loving Savior—one God, not four.

So let the study become worship. Let worship become trust. Let trust become obedience. Let obedience become love. And when the heart grows dull again, as it will, do not despise the return. Open the Word. Behold Him again. Ask the Spirit to make the knowledge of God more than a memory.

He is worthy—infinitely worthy—of your highest thought, your deepest love, and your most wholehearted devotion.

Now go and live as those who have seen Him.

To the God who simply is, who reigns freely, who is pure through and through, and who gives Himself completely—be glory forever. Amen.
